How it is calculated: each sector starts at its share of total S&P 500 market value (the "market weight"). Sectors reading Below history in the sector ideas section get 1.5 times that weight, sectors reading Above history get 0.5 times, and the rest are unchanged; the weights are then rescaled to add up to 100%.
Track record: in a backtest from September 2022 to 2026 (run October 13, 2026, using sector ETFs), this tilted allocation did slightly worse than simply holding the market weights: it trailed by 0.1% over the following 13 weeks, 0.7% over 26 and 0.8% over 52, and came out ahead on 44%, 36% and 23% of test dates.
This is an arithmetic illustration of the screen's rule, not investment advice. It knows nothing about your goals, time horizon, taxes or existing holdings, and the amount you enter is not stored or sent anywhere.

How they are chosen: the top-ranked sector is the one furthest below its own 5-year median across LTM P/E, EV/EBITDA and EV/Revenue; the bottom-ranked sector is the one furthest above. Each must be more than 5% away from its median on at least two of the three multiples. Trend is context only and does not change the ranking. Headlines come from Yahoo Finance for the sector's SPDR ETF, as of the last data pull, and are not shown for past dates.
Track record: in a backtest of these rules from September 2022 to 2026 (run October 13, 2026, using sector ETFs), neither ranking predicted returns. The top-ranked sector trailed the S&P 500 on average over the following 13, 26 and 52 weeks (by 1.6%, 5.2% and 9.3%). The bottom-ranked sector did the opposite of what the ranking implies: it matched the S&P 500 over 13 weeks, beat it by 2.7% over 26 weeks and 2.6% over 52, and beat the average sector on 70% of test dates over 26 weeks. The test covers roughly four years and one market regime, in which expensive sectors kept outperforming.
This is the output of a mechanical screen for study purposes, not investment advice, and it takes no account of anyone's circumstances.
